Dynamic scheduling for type inference

Cornell University · Advisors: Nate Foster & Jules Jacobs

I researched how dynamically scheduling type inference constraints can generalize the heuristics used by Quick Look and FreezeML. CurrentSpring 2026–present

Latent reasoning

Cornell Math+AI Lab · Advisor: Daniel Halpern-Leistner

We are investigating a general reasoning architecture that solves problems by iteratively refining continuous hidden states. The goal is to reuse a simple architecture across tasks, give harder inputs more computation, and let each reasoning step build on earlier steps. The lab explores this approach on tasks including Sudoku, maze solving, and graph algorithms.

My work focuses on recurrent distillation: teaching a smaller model to reproduce a larger reasoning model’s hidden-state trajectory without generating intermediate reasoning tokens. I built the teacher-trace extraction pipeline, a prototype recurrent student, and a Sudoku data pipeline, and I am continuing this work in the 2026–27 academic year.

Fall 2024

Language-agnostic code embeddings

Cornell University · Research course with Marten van Schijndel

With Destiny Vargas and Adler Weber, I investigated whether removing language-specific components from code embeddings improves semantic code retrieval. I generated CodeT5+ pooler embeddings from The Stack and built a CodeSearchNet data loader and text-to-code retrieval evaluation, using cosine similarity and mean reciprocal rank across Python, Java, PHP, and JavaScript.

Our CodeT5+ evaluation found slightly worse retrieval after centering, consistent with the original paper’s result for this model. We also proposed extending the analysis to programming paradigms.
